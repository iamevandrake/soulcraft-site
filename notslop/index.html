First, the important part

Slop is not a synonym for AI

This gets muddled constantly, and the muddle is what makes most anti-slop tools useless. Slop is not a technology. Slop is content nobody stands behind.

A person can write slop by hand: the padded listicle, the SEO page that answers nothing, the press release stretched to fill a word count. And a careful writer can use every model available and publish something worth a reader's time.

The tell was never the tool. It is whether anybody was accountable for what went out. That is what the badge claims, and the button is how a reader tests the claim.

Slop
  • Published at volume, reviewed by nobody
  • Answers a keyword instead of a question
  • Three hundred words of preamble before the point
  • Confident about things it did not check
  • No author, or an author who never read it
Not slop
  • Someone put their name on it and meant it
  • Gets to the point while the reader is still here
  • Says something the reader could not get elsewhere
  • Admits the parts that are uncertain
  • Gets fixed when a reader shows it is wrong
Install

One line, then it just works

Paste it wherever you want the badge to appear. Reports arrive as email. That is the whole setup.

<!-- Reports land in your inbox -->
<script src="https://soulcraftagency.com/notslop/v1.js"
        data-notslop
        data-to="you@yoursite.com"></script>
One

The claim

The badge appears where you dropped the script. It reads Not Slop, which is a statement you are making in public about your own page.

Two

The challenge

Next to it sits a quiet link: seems like slop? A reader who disagrees picks a reason, adds a note, and sends it. No account, no login.

Three

The loop

It reaches you, not a ranking system. You read it. If it holds up, you fix the page. A badge nobody can challenge is worth nothing.

Routing

Your reports, your inbox

Soulcraft never sees them. There is no database on our end, because there is no database.

Email

Simplest path. The reader's mail client opens with everything prefilled. Nothing to host.

data-to="you@site.com"

Your endpoint

POSTs clean JSON to any URL you control. Wire it to Slack, a webhook, Airtable, whatever you already use.

data-endpoint="/api/slop"

Nowhere

Configure neither and it runs in demo mode. Reports stay in the reader's browser. Useful for trying it out.

Reference

Every option

All optional. The defaults are the ones we would pick for you.

AttributeDefaultWhat it does
data-tononeEmail address that receives reports. Opens the reader's mail client with the report prefilled.
data-endpointnoneURL that receives a JSON POST. Takes priority over data-to. Uses sendBeacon, falls back to fetch.
data-labelNot SlopThe claim on the badge. Try Written by a human or Reviewed by a person.
data-trigger-labelSeems like slop?The challenge text next to the claim.
data-themeautoauto, light, or dark. Auto follows the reader's system setting.
data-positioninlineinline puts it where the script sits. corner pins it bottom-right.
data-accentterracottaAny CSS color, so the badge matches your palette instead of ours.

Want it somewhere specific? Add data-notslop-mount to any element and the badge renders inside it. For single-page apps, call NotSlop.mount() yourself and append the returned host wherever you like.

Where this came from

LinkedIn had the right idea and the wrong shape

In July 2026, LinkedIn added a "seems like AI slop" button to every post. The reports feed classifiers that quietly demote low-quality content in the feed. Inside a closed platform with a ranking algorithm, that works. There are teeth.

Copy it straight onto the open web and it falls apart. A publisher who bolts a complaint box onto their own pages is inviting insults with nothing on the other end. The people who would install it are the people who least need it, and the people who need it never will.

So we turned it around. The claim comes first, which is why a publisher installs it at all. The challenge is what makes the claim mean anything. And the report goes home to the person who can actually fix the page.

A badge nobody can dispute is a sticker. A badge anyone can dispute is a standard.

Questions

The obvious ones

Does this mean the page was written without AI?

No, and that matters. The badge claims a person is accountable for the page, not that no software touched it. We build content systems for a living and we use these tools every day. The line we hold is that a human reads, edits, and signs off on what goes out. Anyone claiming the tool itself is the problem has not looked closely at the slop humans produce unassisted.

Where do the reports go?

Wherever you point them. Email with data-to, your own URL with data-endpoint, or nowhere at all if you configure neither. Soulcraft never receives your reports and there is no account to create. The script makes zero network requests unless you give it an endpoint.

Can competitors brigade my page?

The widget limits each reader to one report per page per day, carries a hidden honeypot field, and throws away anything submitted faster than a person could type. More to the point, reports are private notes to you rather than a public score, so there is nothing to game. No leaderboard means no incentive to attack it.

Will it slow down or break my site?

5.7KB gzipped over the wire, no dependencies, and it renders inside a Shadow DOM. Your CSS cannot reach into it and it cannot leak out into yours. We tested that by throwing * { font-family: Comic Sans MS !important } at it. The source ships unminified on purpose, so you can read every line before you trust it.

Is it really free?

Yes, and MIT licensed. Download the file and self-host it if you would rather not depend on us being online. That is not a loophole, it is the intended use.

Why would I invite criticism of my own content?

Because the invitation is the signal. Anyone can write "human-written" in a footer. Very few will put a working complaint button next to it. The button is the proof, and readers can tell the difference.

The badge is free. The standard is the work.
