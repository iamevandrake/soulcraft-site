The Discipline

What we mean by SEO

SEO stands for Superintelligence Engine Optimization now. The acronym didn't change; the engines did. Google answers questions above its own results. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity search the web and cite the sources they trust. Getting found means getting ranked, getting cited and getting recommended, across all of them, and it is one discipline. We wrote up why.

The fundamentals are the ones that always worked, done properly: clear entities, real authors with real credentials, original research, structured data, fast crawlable pages, and content that answers the question before the reader scrolls. The finish line is different. We measure whether the engines mention you, cite you and recommend you, alongside the rankings and traffic you've always tracked.

Going Deeper

The three layers of modern search visibility

If you're thinking about search in 2026, you need to think in three layers. Each one rewards slightly different work, and the businesses getting found are the ones treating all three as part of a single discipline.

Traditional SEO: the foundation

Classic search engine optimization hasn't gone away, it has evolved. The fundamentals still matter: semantic HTML structure, fast load times, mobile responsiveness, quality backlinks, and comprehensive content that demonstrates expertise. Google still indexes and ranks pages, and organic search still drives the largest share of website traffic for most businesses. What's changed is the bar for quality. Thin content that might have ranked five years ago gets buried today. Search engines have gotten remarkably good at identifying depth, originality, and genuine expertise. We audit and fix the technical foundation first -- server response times, crawlability, structured data, internal linking -- so that every page you publish starts from a position of technical strength.

AIO: showing up in AI Overviews

Google's AI Overviews now appear at the top of the majority of informational search queries. This AI-generated summary box pulls from web content to provide a synthesized answer, and earning a citation in that box has become the new equivalent of ranking in the top position. The content that gets cited tends to share specific characteristics: clear definitions early in the page, comprehensive FAQ sections, structured data that machines can parse, and an authoritative tone backed by specifics rather than vague claims. We write every page to check each of these boxes -- not as an afterthought, but as the core architecture of the page.

GEO: generative engine optimization

Beyond Google, a growing ecosystem of AI-powered search platforms -- ChatGPT search, Perplexity, Gemini, and others -- are becoming primary discovery channels for millions of users. These generative engines don't show a list of links; they generate answers and cite their sources inline. Optimizing for these platforms, known as GEO, requires a different approach than traditional SEO. Entity-rich content, structured data, comparison-ready information, and content that's been updated within the last three months all increase the probability of being selected as a cited source. We monitor the engines continuously and refresh the pages that matter at the pace these models update their retrieval pools.

Three layers, one engine, one discipline. We don't sell them separately because they aren't separate.
