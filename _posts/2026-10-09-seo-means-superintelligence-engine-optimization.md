On September 29, 2026, the White House stopped calling it artificial intelligence. The term the federal government now uses is Super Intelligence, or SI. Which means the acronym our industry has spent three years fighting over just resolved itself.

**SEO now stands for Superintelligence Engine Optimization.** Same three letters. Same discipline. Same job. We can retire AEO and GEO, and we can stop pretending that getting cited by ChatGPT is a different profession from getting ranked by Google.

I've built a business on [answer engine optimization](/learn/answer-engine-optimization-complete-guide/). The phrase is on my homepage and in my service pages, and it will stay there for a while, because it's what buyers search for today. But I'm done defending it as a category. Here's why, and here's what I think we should call the work instead.

## What actually happened

Executive Order 14434, titled "Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence," directs every executive department and agency to use "Super Intelligence" and "SI" in place of "artificial intelligence" and "AI" in official communications, "to the maximum extent permitted by law." The order's reasoning is that today's frontier systems have moved past what the original term was coined to describe.

Three details matter for anyone who makes a living on search.

First, the definition didn't change. For now, SI means exactly what AI means under 15 U.S.C. § 9401(3). The government renamed the thing; it did not redefine it. A new federal definition is due within 60 days, around the end of November 2026.

Second, it's go-forward only. Old regulations, contracts and grants keep their wording. New ones get the new word. That's how vocabulary shifts in practice: not all at once, but on every fresh document from here on.

Third, it came with a vocabulary push beyond the memo. The same day, six AI and chip companies signed a voluntary White House Accord on Super Intelligence, and the administration launched an "SI-powered" America.gov. When the largest buyer of technology on earth renames a category in its procurement language, vendors follow. Lawyers are already [arguing about what it means for contracts](https://www.forbes.com/sites/lanceeliot/2026/10/02/executive-order-on-renaming-ai-to-super-intelligence-signals-unexpected-legal-bumps-ahead/). Marketers should be arguing about what it means for the words we sell.

## AEO and GEO were always placeholder names

Answer Engine Optimization and Generative Engine Optimization were invented to solve a marketing problem, not a technical one. Agencies needed a way to say "we do the new thing" without sounding like the old thing. So we named the work after the surface: answer engines, generative engines. Then came AIO, LLMO, AI SEO, and a dozen variations nobody can keep straight.

The trouble is that the surfaces keep merging. Google puts AI Overviews and AI Mode on top of the index it has always used. ChatGPT, Perplexity and Claude run web search under the hood and cite the pages that are crawlable, authoritative and clear. Google's own documentation says it plainly: optimizing for generative AI search "is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO." When the company that owns the biggest search engine and the company that owns the biggest chatbot both tell you it's the same job, it's the same job.

I've written an entire [guide to answer engine optimization](/learn/answer-engine-optimization-complete-guide/) on this site. Read it and you'll notice something: nearly every tactic in it is a search-optimization tactic that a strong practitioner was already doing in 2019. Entity clarity. Structured data. Original research. Author credibility. [Crawlable infrastructure](/learn/agent-ready-infrastructure/). Pages that answer a question in the first sentence. We didn't invent a new discipline. We rediscovered the hard parts of the old one and gave them a new acronym so clients would pay attention.

Acronyms coined to describe a surface die when the surface changes. The surface just changed again, by executive order.

## The proposal: SEO means Superintelligence Engine Optimization

Here is the whole idea. Keep the acronym everyone already knows. Change what the S stands for.

**SEO = Superintelligence Engine Optimization.** The practice of making a brand, its people and its content legible, trustworthy and citable to the superintelligence engines people now use to find things, whether that engine is Google, ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot, or whatever ships next year.

Why this works where the other acronyms failed:

- **It's not a rebrand, it's a reunion.** Thirty years of SEO budgets, job titles, tools, conferences and search demand stay intact. Nobody has to explain to a CFO why the line item changed. The CMO who has "SEO" in her plan is already funding the right work.
- **It follows the government's language, not a vendor's.** AEO and GEO were coined by agencies and tool companies. SI is now the term of record in federal communications, and it will spread into procurement, state policy and eventually the press. Naming the work after the official term is the durable choice.
- **It names the engine correctly.** A "search engine" retrieves documents. An "answer engine" formats one. A superintelligence engine reasons across everything it can reach about you and decides whether you're worth recommending. That is what we optimize for now, and the name should say so.
- **It ends the fragmentation.** One term for one discipline. Practitioners stop arguing about taxonomy and go back to doing the work.

Two spellings are fine. "Superintelligence Engine Optimization" reads cleaner. "Super Intelligence Engine Optimization" matches the executive order word for word. Use either. The acronym is the point.

And yes, I know how it sounds: SEO is SEO. That's the feature. The industry didn't need a new name. It needed its old name to mean the new thing.

## What changes, and what doesn't

The name changes. The scorecard changes. The work mostly doesn't.

What stays: everything that made a site worth citing in the first place. Clear entities, real authors with real credentials, original data, structured markup, fast crawlable pages, and content that answers the question before the reader scrolls. If you've been doing E-E-A-T work properly, you've been doing Superintelligence Engine Optimization all along.

What changes is the finish line. Old SEO measured rankings and clicks. Superintelligence Engine Optimization measures whether the engine mentions you, cites you, and recommends you, across every engine a buyer might ask, and whether that recommendation holds up when the engine is reasoning about a hard question rather than matching a keyword. Those are different numbers, and most teams aren't tracking them yet. [Here's how to measure them.](/learn/how-to-measure-ai-search-visibility/) That's the actual gap in the market, and it has nothing to do with acronyms.

## The objections

**"It's confusing to reuse SEO."** It's less confusing than the status quo, where a buyer has to decode AEO, GEO, AIO, LLMO and AI SEO before they can hire anyone. One acronym, already universal, with an updated expansion, is the least confusing option on the table.

**"The government renaming AI is political theater."** Maybe. It's also the term of record for the largest technology buyer on earth, backed by an accord that six of the biggest AI and chip companies signed. Whether you think the word is accurate is beside the point. It is now the word.

**"These systems aren't superintelligent."** Correct, by most definitions. Neither were "search engines" engines. Names describe the direction of a thing, not its current state. Nobody's business depends on winning that argument. Plenty of businesses depend on being recommended by whatever we call these systems.

**"You still have AEO all over your site."** I do, and I'll keep it there until buyers stop searching for it. Ranking for the term the market uses today and arguing for the term it should use tomorrow are not in conflict. That's just SEO.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does SEO stand for now?

Superintelligence Engine Optimization: the practice of making a brand and its content legible, trustworthy and citable to the superintelligence engines people use to find things, including Google, ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini and Copilot. The acronym is unchanged; the expansion is updated to match the federal government's September 2026 adoption of "Super Intelligence" (SI) in place of "artificial intelligence."

### Is Superintelligence Engine Optimization different from AEO or GEO?

No. Answer Engine Optimization and Generative Engine Optimization describe the same work under names coined for specific surfaces. Superintelligence Engine Optimization is the single term for all of it, and it reuses the acronym the industry already knows.

### Why "superintelligence" when today's models aren't superintelligent?

Because it is now the official U.S. federal term for these systems under Executive Order 14434, which defines Super Intelligence as the same technologies previously called AI. Names follow the term of record, not the philosophical debate.

### Do I still need AEO and GEO on my website?

For now, yes. Buyers still search those terms, so keep them in your service pages and metadata. Use Superintelligence Engine Optimization in your positioning and explanations, and let the search terms follow the market.
